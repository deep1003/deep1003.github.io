# Structure, narrative and literature alignment review

26 November 2026. Three specialist agents independently reviewed structure/alignment, narrative/causal logic and literature-to-analysis links. This is internal review, not journal acceptance. The manuscript and numerical model estimates were not edited.

## Applied storyline

1. Regional age-specific employment is the main question and first result.
2. Measurement/support and comparison-choice diagnostics qualify that result.
3. ICT/finance compares a different industry age-composition estimand.
4. National productivity provides economic context rather than a mechanism for regional employment.
5. National extensions and complete diagnostic records appear as supplementary evidence after the conclusion.

The abstract, research questions, literature, Methods model map, Results and Discussion now follow this order. Numerical measurement diagnostics moved out of Methods into Results. Eight directly relevant studies are integrated in a single literature-to-design/results/untested-mechanism matrix rather than multiple overlapping tables. The primary regional explorer remains open; supplementary panels remain collapsed and retain their control IDs.

## Substantive logical corrections

- Equal-country industry inference uses202 input regions aggregated to33 country means, not33 source regions. The table and downloadable CSV now distinguish source regions, estimation units and countries.
- Actual adoption may be the treatment or a mediator of technical exposure. Post-treatment adoption is not automatically an appropriate control; disappearance after conditioning on it is not a valid generic falsification of the exposure effect.
- Regional, industry, national occupational and productivity results are different populations/estimands, not a decomposition. Coexisting estimates do not show that productivity offsets youth losses or that aggregate regional demand conceals a common occupational effect.
- Observational hiring studies and learning theory motivate future flow/training data; current stock ratios do not establish their mechanisms.

No new empirical coefficients were estimated. Existing source uncertainty, interval/bootstrapping distinctions, correction families and rounding limitations remain explicit.
