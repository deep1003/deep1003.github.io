# Tracking worker adaptation after potential AI shocks

Source and access assessment checked on 22 November 2026. The study concerns all workers, with emphasis on early career and dimensions of economic vulnerability. Its distinctive contribution should concern adjustment paths rather than treating potential exposure as equivalent to actual displacement.

## What is feasible now

Validated linked CPS is the most immediate route to monthly full-time-to-part-time, involuntary part-time, unemployment and non-participation transitions. We have implemented an exploratory adjacent-month version from existing official files in this extension. Households rotate through four interviews, eight months out and four more interviews. Person identifiers must be checked against demographics and rotation position. Movers are not followed as a conventional person panel would follow them. Longitudinal weights are preferable to simply retaining cross-sectional weights. [IPUMS linking documentation](https://cps.ipums.org/cps/cps_linking_documentation.shtml).

The implemented exercise uses January–February through August–September pairs in each year, validates identifiers, sex, race, age progression, state and month-in-sample progression, and exports aggregate counts only. Age and occupational exposure refer to the origin observation, while the destination can be any occupation. Origin-weighted rates are conditional on successfully linked respondents. They are exploratory evidence, not an official gross-flow series, an attrition-corrected population estimate or proof that a change was forced. Identifier changes, attrition and unstable transition classification remain concerns. [Rodgers and Flood's linkage method](https://assets.ipums.org/_files/ipums/working_papers/ipums_wp_2023-01.pdf); [BLS gross-flow research](https://www.bls.gov/osmr/research-papers/2024/pdf/st240100.pdf).

SIPP is the strongest complementary public survey for longer monthly job histories, hours, earnings, household resources and benefit coverage. The verified 2025 SIPP release became available in July 2026 and concerns reference-period 2024 outcomes. It does not furnish completed 2026 employment histories. Cohorts spanning before and after November 2022 should be selected using panel identifiers and overlap, not by stacking unrelated survey years. We collected variable candidates from the 2025 API metadata but did not estimate SIPP trajectories in this extension. [Census release](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/2025-sipp.html); [SIPP content](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/sipp/about/sipp-content-information.html).

## Contract security requires a separate measurement axis

Full-time and permanent employment are different concepts. A full-time worker can be hired for a project, and a part-time worker can hold an ongoing job. Independent contracting, temporary-help-agency work, on-call work and employment through a contract firm also differ. A US ongoing employee job does not necessarily confer the security implied by a Korean regular-employment category.

The CPS Contingent Work Supplement directly measures several relevant arrangements. The verified July 2023 instrument distinguishes expected job duration from alternative arrangements. Its May 2017 predecessor offers a historical comparison, but these intermittent snapshots cannot identify the same person's permanent-to-contract transition across 2017–2023. Questionnaire changes and the pandemic complicate an exposure × post contrast. BLS cautions against state tabulations because samples are small. [BLS definitions and comparability](https://www.bls.gov/cps/labor-force/contingent-and-alternative-arrangements-faqs-2023.htm).

The SIPP metadata screen found that variables named EJB*_CNTRC refer to union-contract coverage. They must not be interpreted as fixed-term or contingent contracts. Suitable duration, arrangement or reason-for-job-ending variables need questionnaire and universe validation before a contract-transition analysis can be promised. A reason indicating a temporary job ended does not by itself classify every employed month as permanent or temporary. [2025 SIPP variable catalogue](https://api.census.gov/data/2025/sipp/variables.html).

## Administrative flows and state partnerships

Public QWI and J2J are useful complements rather than replacements for individual survey histories. QWI supplies hires, separations and earnings for demographic and industry groups; J2J describes employment moves, origin/destination jobs and earnings changes. Public tabulations cannot reconstruct identifiable people, and do not generally supply weekly hours or contract permanence. QWI age bands do not exactly reproduce 22–25, 26–29 and 30–34. Restricted LEHD can support longer worker–employer histories through approved FSRDC projects, subject to available vintages, approval and disclosure rules. Approval has not been sought or obtained here. [Census product and access documentation](https://lehd.ces.census.gov/data/); [restricted LEHD](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/ces/data/restricted-use-data/lehd-data.html).

Washington is a promising hours-and-earnings case study because its wage system records hours as well as earnings. This can help distinguish reduced labour supply from reduced hourly pay. Confidential data need an authorised agreement; reported quarterly hours and industry assignments have limitations and still do not directly reveal contract permanence. The state handbook and data-sharing route were located, though full endpoint access was blocked during this audit. New Jersey's research office provides another route and documents enhanced-wage-record research; that report is not evidence that every desired variable is already operational. [Washington data-sharing route](https://esd.wa.gov/about-us/data-sharing-requests); [Washington P20W handbook](https://erdc.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2024-11/ERDC_P20W_Data_Handbook_Final.pdf); [New Jersey research office](https://www.nj.gov/labor/research-info/ori/wra/).

Retraining is another adaptation path. Hyman, Lahey, Ni and Pilossoph's New York Fed study links WIOA programme spells to earnings and compares training with job-search assistance. It provides a relevant model for studying adjustment among displaced and comparatively low-income workers. Its 2012–2023 programme data neither represent all workers nor identify adjustment to the 2025–2026 agentic-AI wave. Programme selection and access to linked records must be addressed. [New York Fed Staff Report 1165](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr1165.html).

## Other panels and reporting

PSID is attractive for longer family-resource and benefit histories, but young state-by-occupation cells are small and its interview frequency is less suited to immediate monthly transitions. The verified TAS-2025 early release explicitly excludes employment data, so its existence must not be presented as an available new youth-employment panel. NLSY97 has rich weekly histories but follows people born in 1980–1984, who were approximately 38–42 when ChatGPT launched; it is useful for midcareer adjustment, not contemporary 22–25-year-old entry. [PSID release caveat](https://simba.isr.umich.edu/Zips/EarlyRelease.aspx); [NLS employment histories](https://nlsinfo.org/content/cohorts/nlsy97/topical-guide/employment/introduction).

KUOW's November 2026 interviews illustrate why contract security and benefits belong in the framework. One interviewee describes a post-layoff contract without typical employee benefits; another combines part-time seasonal jobs without benefits. These cases motivate outcomes, but establish neither population prevalence nor AI causality. Axios provides additional context. The state report linked in that coverage was located, but an implausible GDP-unit statement in its indexed excerpt was not reused. [KUOW reporting](https://www.kuow.org/economy/2026-11-09/its-a-really-tough-market-11-washingtonians-weigh-in-on-the-jobs-outlook); [Axios context](https://www.axios.com/local/seattle/2026/11/17/seattle-tech-layoffs-job-market-washington-2026).

## Measurement strategy for unequal adaptation

The current exploratory dimensions are no bachelor's degree, female, Black alone, Hispanic and foreign-born workers, separately for all adults and ages 22–34. These are overlapping dimensions, not a claim that every member is disadvantaged or that group differences identify discrimination. Current low earnings should not define a subgroup after the outcome has changed. A stronger vulnerability design would fix pre-shock earnings/resources, disability, caregiving, digital access and local alternatives using longitudinal baseline records, retaining intersectional groups only where support permits.

An informative trajectory has at least five states, ongoing employee work, contingent/alternative-arrangement work, involuntary part-time work, unemployment and non-participation. Hours, contract security, benefits and earnings should remain separate dimensions rather than being forced into one ordinal ladder. Re-employment can involve a pay cut, fewer hours, lost benefits or a beneficial voluntary change. Financial pressure and stated reasons help distinguish constrained adjustment from preference.

Next work should validate attrition-adjusted CPS links, construct pre-2022 SIPP cohorts, and assess a Washington or restricted-LEHD collaboration. Actual adoption or an independently justified shock is needed for a stronger causal interpretation. Our current event is common public availability, and our exposure measures are retrospective potential scores.

Full-time/part-time status uses the CPS usual full-time labour-force recode PRFTLF. Economic-reason part-time uses PRWKSTAT codes 3 and 6, which include usual full-time workers temporarily working short hours for economic reasons. It can therefore overlap usual full-time status and is not a subset of the usual part-time group. An FT-to-economic-PT observation can be a temporary hours cut rather than a move into a usually part-time job.


## Adaptive capacity and observed adaptation

Manning and Aguirre (2026) already study prospective adaptive capacity, combining financial buffers, age, local labour-market density and skill transferability. The Brookings account by Manning, Aguirre, Muro and Methkupally explains that framework. Our contribution should therefore be framed as observed employment-quality and transition patterns under comparable exposure measures, not the first study of adaptation. Their capacity index is not itself evidence of realised AI-induced transitions. [Primary institutional account](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/measuring-us-workers-capacity-to-adapt-to-ai-driven-job-displacement/).
