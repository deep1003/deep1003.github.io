Methods
We independently aggregate official CPS employment counts and link occupations to our frozen THAIE classification. Six exact age groups are retained. Employment indices use November 2022 = 100 and display November 2022 onward; they are not employment rates or causal effects.
Employment-index charts display November 2022 onward. DID validation retains the pre-period from December 2018 and uses symmetric windows around the excluded November 2022 transition month. The 36-month window is a robustness check. Regressions use observed months only.
Full data construction, support and estimation methods
Population and age groups.
CPS estimates cover employed civilian noninstitutional people aged 16 and older in the 50 states and DC. The six focal groups are 22–25, 26–30, 31–34, 35–40, 41–49, and 50+. The all-worker series also includes ages 16–21. Employed people include those at work and those temporarily absent. Composite final weights produce employment counts, not employment-to-population rates. Full-time and part-time follow the CPS labor-force classification and do not identify permanent versus temporary contracts. Recorded sex is reported as women and men.
Exposure.
Occupational THAIE quintiles use frozen 2019 employment-based membership. Quintiles are not recalculated within age, sex, or employment-status groups. Scores are retrospective task-based measures, not observed employer adoption. Unmapped occupations remain unassigned.

Display coverage and support.
The underlying descriptive file spans January 2020–October 2026. The active charts display November 2022 onward. A displayed observation requires at least 30 employed sample records and sufficient underlying 2022 support, including at least 120 person-month records. A series also requires a supported, positive November 2022 baseline. Missing or low-support values remain unavailable. October 2025 is linearly interpolated between supported September and November observations for display only and drawn with dashed lines. Curves are not seasonally adjusted. Employment stocks do not identify hiring, separations, or individual transitions.
Within-occupation comparisons.
Software developers are Census occupation 1021 and THAIE Q4 in this mapping. A fixed occupation score cannot define five exposure strata inside one detailed occupation. The age buttons select one age-specific employment curve while occupation remains fixed. The broader computer and mathematical family can contain multiple exposure groups. Age does not directly measure experience or seniority.
Current symmetric-window DID and event-study design
The primary pre-period is December 2018–October 2022 and the post-period is December 2022–October 2026. November 2022 is excluded as the transition month. Each side spans 47 calendar months, with 47 observed pre-months and 46 observed post-months because October 2025 is unavailable. The 36-month robustness window uses November 2019–October 2022 and December 2022–November 2025, with 36 and 35 observed months. No interpolated value enters a regression. Earlier 2017-start specifications remain explicitly archived.
Age-specific Poisson quasi-likelihood models estimate Q5 versus Q1–Q4 contrasts in weighted full-time or part-time counts. Fixed effects are occupation×industry, occupation×month-of-year, and industry×calendar-date. Separate Q5 interactions cover pandemic quarters in 2020–2021. Earlier occupational codes are probabilistically bridged to the common classification. Industry controls do not remove every occupation-specific or local shock.
Main FT and PT models share supported rows within each age and window. For ages 22–25 in the long window, this retains 91.75% of weighted FT and 99.48% of PT employment. FT-only sensitivity restores all covered FT employment. Zero survey counts in supported model cells are not administrative proof of zero jobs. The descriptive chart threshold is not imposed on estimation.
Reported intervals use occupation-score cluster covariance and are working model-based intervals, not complete CPS survey-design intervals. The 12 primary age-by-outcome contrasts receive Holm correction. Event studies use monthly observations in three-month relative-time bins, with August–October 2022 as the omitted reference. Pandemic quarters have separate nuisance coefficients and are excluded from the flat-lead joint test. Intervals are pointwise rather than simultaneous; boundary bins can contain fewer months.
Population controls, rotation overlap, historical crosswalk error, retrospective exposure, support selection, and unobserved AI adoption remain limitations. Numerical reproduction does not establish parallel trends or a causal AI effect. The current results and their sensitivities are reported together rather than selected by statistical significance.
The opening map pools observed CPS months into three-year calendar windows and reports Q5 full-time employment divided by all same-age residents. The independent ACS check compares 2017–2019 with 2023–2024 and uses past-year usual-hours proxies. Linked CPS separates entry outcomes from incumbent transitions; SIPP observes personal histories through 2024. These distinct measures are not pooled into one causal estimate.
New survey and administrative validation methods
ACS validation uses 2017, 2018, 2019, 2023, and 2024 national person files, exact ages 22–25, all states/DC, frozen THAIE thresholds, and 80 replicate weights. The 2017 occupation bridge contributes fractional expected group weights. Three-year pre and two-year post averages use separately normalized annual weights. Pointwise intervals assume independent annual replicate blocks and condition on the score and bridge. ACS hours and salary refer to the past year, not the current CPS schedule or hourly wage.
The earlier descriptive linked-CPS module uses 79 genuine adjacent-month pairs from 2020–October 2026, rotation eligibility, validated person matches, and cross-fitted response IPW with trimming sensitivity. Origin occupation defines incumbent exposure; destination occupation classifies entry outcomes from a common nonemployment denominator. Neither unknown prior occupation nor missing response is coded as low exposure. The pooled transition periods differ in length and include pandemic effects, so they are descriptive rather than controlled DID estimates.
SIPP uses official two-, three-, and four-year longitudinal weights. Monthly hours intensity is distinguished from usual FT/PT, and general permanent-to-contract status is not inferred from union coverage or restricted-universe contract questions. QWI/J2J use quarterly industry exposure and published ages 22–24/35–64, not exact occupational Q5 or ages 22–25. Industry-cluster inference, Holm correction, trend sensitivity, and an extended 2017 preperiod accompany the administrative models.

Controlled linked-CPS entry DID and event-study methods
The full inventory contains 115 genuine adjacent-month pairs and 95,862 jobless-origin transition opportunities at ages 22–25 from February 2017–October 2026. The main paired-outcome model uses 70,229 opportunities in the aligned 2019-start windows. These are repeated transition observations, not independent people. Exact origin age defines eligibility, including people who turn 26 before the next interview.
FT entry requires destination PEMLR 1/2 and PRFTLF 1. It includes wage/salary and self-employed work and is not restricted to first jobs or payroll hiring. The common denominator includes unemployment and nonparticipation. Destination THAIE defines the entry outcome, not treatment assigned to a nonworker. Prior industry is separately used as work-history information in the additive model. Unavailable occupations and histories remain explicit; they are not recoded as low exposure.
The proportional model uses PPML with state×group, destination-industry×group, state×month, industry×month, and group×season effects plus group-specific pandemic-quarter controls. The additive model regresses the paired Q5-minus-Q1–Q4 entry indicator on post and origin-state, seasonal, prior-industry and labor-force-history controls. Response models use only origin information with household-fold cross-fitting. Covariance alternatives and the distinction between pointwise inference and causal identification are reported with the results.
Quarter-specific event coefficients require four seasonal reference quarters in 2022 to coexist with group-specific monthly seasonality. State-plus-quarter covariance is indefinite for the full pretrend blocks and some event intervals. Those tests are withheld; plotted state-cluster intervals are conditional diagnostics. No interpolation enters the models.
