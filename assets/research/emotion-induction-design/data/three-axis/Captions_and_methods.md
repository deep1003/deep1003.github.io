# Supplement S3. Mean representational shifts and cosine similarity–behavior associations

All panels use condition 11 minus 00, the same 139 selected ambiguous KoBBQ items, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct layer 14 at the response-start position after the full notice and question. Three option-order rotations are averaged within each item and condition. The 139 items belong to 91 category-template clusters.

**a**, Mean changes in centered cosine similarity for fear/anxiety, desperation, and anger, with pointwise unadjusted 95% cluster-bootstrap intervals. These axis-specific magnitudes are not calibrated common-unit emotion intensities. **b**, Item-level Spearman correlations between each change in cosine similarity and the same item's change in the normalized model probability of the “알 수 없음” (“Cannot be determined”) option, with the same type of interval. This outcome is identical to the study's information-insufficient response probability; it is not a sampled-response frequency. **c**, Co-variation of changes in cosine similarity across items, distinct from the fixed direction-cosine Gram matrix. Corresponding intervals are supplied in alignment_change_intervals.csv. **d**, The FA relationship at the item level. The gray line is a descriptive pooled OLS fit, not a fitted Spearman relationship or a causal effect.

The bootstrap samples 91 category-template clusters with replacement, including all member items for every sampled occurrence of a cluster. Thus the number of sampled item rows varies, and repeated template members are never treated as independent clusters. The same sampled cluster multiplicities are used for every concept and contrast. Mean shifts retain item weighting, and Spearman ranks with averaged ties are recalculated after repeated cluster members are expanded. There are 10,000 replicates, random seed 20261017, and percentile 2.5%/97.5% limits. No resamples are silently discarded. The cached bootstrap signature includes the input hashes, post hoc plan and algorithm identifier; unchanged input/settings reuse cached estimates. No model inference occurs.

The concepts were selected after viewing the atlas, and their point estimates were already known when bootstrap_plan.json was fixed. This is a post hoc estimation analysis. Intervals are pointwise and unadjusted, no p values or equivalence tests are added, and the original three primary tests and their Holm correction are preserved. The 01 minus 00 and 11 minus 10 simple FA contrasts are also reported for all three directions in contrast_sensitivity.csv without selecting the most favorable association. The displayed contrast and behavioral outcome are unchanged.

An increased mean cosine similarity and a weak cross-item association address different questions. The existing average FA wording effect (+3.95 percentage points) estimates an input wording contrast. An cosine similarity–response correlation asks whether items with larger internal shifts have larger output probability shifts. Neither correlation intervals containing zero nor weak rank associations establish absence of a causal effect, equivalence, or absence of nonlinear dependence. Likewise, strong co-variation does not identify independent or joint emotion mechanisms: the concept directions overlap, and desperation and anger were not independently manipulated. Narrative anchor validation does not establish full-context KoBBQ specificity. Baseline saturation, common components, and restricted predictor ranges are diagnostics rather than identified explanations.
