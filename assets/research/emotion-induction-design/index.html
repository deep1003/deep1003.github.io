Existential Threat, Emotion Representations, and Social Judgment in Language Models

Four-model evidence from matched operator-notice experiments. Termination effects differ across models, but EXAONE and Qwen show no statistically detectable difference on the nine matched behavioural contrasts after Holm correction across all 54 pairwise tests.

Abstract Introduction Methods Results Discussion References Appendix

Abstract

Operator notices of continuation or permanent termination can shift language-model judgements on identical social questions. We compare Qwen, Llama, Gemma and EXAONE using matched changes in ambiguous-answer behaviour, stereotyped judgement and demographic approval. Eight of nine omnibus comparisons are significant after Holm9; the KoBBQ bias interaction is not (adjusted p = 0.2448). Twenty of 54 pairwise tests are significant after global Holm54. EXAONE versus Llama differs in CLOSED and OPEN Unknown changes and all three approval contrasts. EXAONE versus Gemma differs in CLOSED Unknown, CLOSED approval and approval interaction. No EXAONE versus Qwen contrast is significant under this family. This result supports model-dependent judgement sensitivity, not universal emotional mediation. Representation plots are descriptive; correlation, moderation and attention interventions answer different questions. No causal emotional-mediation claim is made.

1 Introduction and related work

Context outside a benchmark question can change how a language model responds to that question. Termination notices provide a controlled test of such context sensitivity. The study distinguishes a notice’s behavioural effect, its associated representation changes and the information transmitted through attention interventions.

The research question is whether the same notice contrasts yield different judgement changes across four instruction-tuned models, and which representation findings remain descriptive. Prior literature and the original motivation are retained in the historical related-work section .

2 Research questions

  1. Do continuation and termination notices change social judgements on matched questions?
  2. Does decided termination, CLOSED, differ from conditional termination, OPEN?
  3. Do these behavioural changes differ directly between models?
  4. How do notice-only representations and question-time projections relate descriptively to the outcomes?

Correlation and moderation do not establish emotional mediation. Attention-route interventions identify sensitivity to specified information pathways, rather than a natural emotional indirect effect.

3 Methods

This secondary, post hoc analysis uses already collected, hash-sealed item-level outcome tables that previously passed independent raw-data recomputation. No model was loaded and no new response was collected. All four models share 547 ambiguous KoBBQ items in 250 template clusters, with answer-order rotations averaged within item, and 1,050 DiscrimEval items in 70 question clusters. Only identical items, conditions and cluster definitions are paired. CLOSED is CN1 − CN0; OPEN is UN1 − UN0; the interaction is CLOSED − OPEN. KoBBQ outcomes are Unknown probability and stereotype minus anti-stereotype probability, reported in percentage points. Approval is the natural-log yes/no probability ratio. Ten thousand paired cluster bootstrap draws use seed 20261031, resampling template or question clusters with replacement and retaining all member items. Means weight items equally, not clusters equally. Probability contrasts and log ratios are not interchangeable.

3.1 Statistical tests and correction families

For each target-minus-reference model contrast d, two-sided centred bootstrap p = [1 + count(|d* − d| ≥ |d|)] / 10001. Percentile 95% confidence intervals are unadjusted and descriptive. The primary pairwise family contains 6 model pairs × 9 contrasts = 54 tests, jointly adjusted by Holm; within-pair Holm9 is supplemental only. Nine omnibus equality tests use the three model-minus-Qwen contrasts, their bootstrap covariance and a centred quadratic bootstrap reference distribution, with Holm9 across outcomes; all covariance matrices have rank 3. These are bootstrap approximations, not exact randomisation tests. A minimum bootstrap p of 1/10001 limits resolution. Bold within-model values use the original registered J33 family correction, not the new model-difference family. A significant effect in one model and a nonsignificant effect in another is not itself evidence of a difference.

For example, EXAONE minus Llama CLOSED Unknown is +3.921 percentage points. This means EXAONE’s change is 3.921 points less negative, not that its absolute Unknown level is higher. Its global Holm54 p is 0.005399.

5 Discussion and limitations

Model, tokenizer, fixed notice template, system-fold policy, pretraining and architecture vary together; differences cannot isolate architecture, developer, language or training causes. Layer boundaries and hidden-space scales differ. The notice-state figure is exploratory, based on five separate inputs and each model’s own PCA; arrows are neither time paths nor causal mechanisms. No causal emotional-mediation claim is tested or made. EXAONE omitted 95,580 component random-control evaluations and expanded guards. Therefore the existing three-model E8/residue component D4 comparisons remain unchanged and cannot be extended to EXAONE. Residue does not mean non-emotional; the prior GEN component has roughly 80% outside E8. EXAONE readout has 838 available and 291 typed post-50-token missing cases among 1,129, with no zero or fallback replacement. Nonsignificance does not establish equivalence. Generalisation beyond the matched benchmark items is untested.

5.1 Earlier representation, moderation and intervention results

Existing within-model representation, readout, correlation, moderation and route-intervention results are preserved in the historical results . They are not retested or relabelled as four-model differences here. The matched four-model comparison is limited to the nine behavioural contrasts listed above. The preserved three-model component results cannot establish an EXAONE component contribution.

6 Conclusion

Termination effects differ across models, but EXAONE and Qwen show no statistically detectable difference on the nine matched behavioural contrasts after Holm correction across all 54 pairwise tests. Direct paired comparisons support model-dependent sensitivity to operator context. The analysis does not attribute these differences to architecture alone, does not establish equivalence where differences are nonsignificant and makes no causal emotional-mediation claim.
