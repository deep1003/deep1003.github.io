Harms Cross Domains: Embodied AI Risks Do Not Stay in One Category Under review

Chun, Y., Park, Y., Yoon, J., Kim, S., Chang, J., Lee, S., Jung, J., & Kim, J. (2026). ACM FAccT 2027.

Risk taxonomies assign each risk to a single domain — general-purpose, agentic or physical — but reported incidents do not respect those boundaries. Mapping incidents onto 622 evidence-linked risk definitions across two independent incident sources, against random baselines controlling for domain size and embedding artefacts, shows that risk definitions stay within their assigned domains while incident mappings cross them: among incidents whose closest card was general-purpose, embodied incidents were 3.56 times as likely to activate agentic or physical risks. The handover of control between automation and people is the main bridge between domains.

Existential Threat, Emotion Representations, and Social Judgment in Language Models Under review

Chun, Y., & Park, Y. (2026). ACM FAccT 2027.

Threat-related messages about a model's continued operation change how it answers questions about other people, with the information about those people held fixed. Confirmed deletion reduces the probability of withholding judgement on ambiguous questions by 2.65 percentage points relative to confirmed continuation. Emotion-related internal representations shift alongside these responses, but the interventions tested do not establish that those representations transmit the effect — a distinction the paper argues must be evidenced separately before a mechanistic claim is made.

Time-Horizon AI Exposure: A Task-Based Measure with Evidence from Korea's Labour Market Submitted

Chun, Y. (2026). KOSIME 2026 Winter Conference, Korea Society of Innovation, Management and Economics. Submitted with UNIST affiliation.

AI exposure is usually measured by how relevant AI is to the tasks workers perform, not by how long those tasks take. Time-Horizon AI Exposure (THAIE) reweights the measure using estimated human task duration and model-specific completion curves, which reorders rankings among highly exposed Korean occupations. Within that group, a one-standard-deviation increase in THAIE is associated with a trend-adjusted employment difference of −4.3 log points after the emergence of generative AI, where unweighted exposure shows no significant break. Exposed employment nevertheless expanded, as gains among older workers outweighed losses among workers under 30. Substituting THAIE for unweighted exposure reduces cross-validated prediction error by 5.3% in Korea and 8.2% in the United States. Exposure is not a fixed attribute of a task; it depends on the time horizon of the technology, so the measure can be updated as capabilities advance.

ARS-Bench: Benchmarking Security Risks Across the Agentic RAG Attack Lifecycle Under review

Choi, C., Kim, E., Lee, K., Chun, Y., Jeong, J., Kim, E., Oh, M., Jang, J., & Chang, B. (2026). ACL Rolling Review, October 2026.

A benchmark that evaluates security risks across the entire agentic RAG attack lifecycle, from adversarial document ingestion to final response generation, structured along five security-critical dimensions: document ingestion, retrieval, reasoning, final response, and efficiency. Tracing attack effects through the lifecycle enables fine-grained diagnosis of where vulnerabilities enter and how defences should be targeted.

A Smile Blinds the Judge: Role-Blind Valence Pooling in Vision-Language Models

Wang, I. W., & Chun, Y. (2026).

Vision-language safety judges pool facial valence without binding emotion to narrative roles: a victim's smile lowers judged danger, while a perpetrator's smile lowers judged malice. The effect remains significant from 3B local models to a 397B frontier model, indicating that scale alone does not supply the missing role binding.
