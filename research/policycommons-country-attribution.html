Policy Commons country attribution

Reproducible protocol for measuring country-level attention to artificial intelligence in Policy Commons documents.

Current release v7.6: 269,853 master records; 182,018 analysis document-country rows; 135 sovereign-country codes. HKG is aggregated to CHN for analysis, while original fields are preserved. Source records are never physically deleted.

Workflow

  1. Freeze RIS, CSV, query, filters, page and checksums.
  2. Parse fields and join RIS–CSV only with normalised Policy Commons and artefact identifiers.
  3. Deduplicate using stable identifiers, canonical URLs and compatible content evidence.
  4. Retain candidates from documented AI-oriented Policy Commons searches. Metadata without an AI term is not Non-AI evidence because matching may occur in indexed full text.
  5. Exclude only clear alternative meanings and standalone non-policy formats.
  6. Classify issuer scope and assign supported country relationships.
  7. Count unique (record_id, analysis_country) rows with unit weight 1.
  8. Run deterministic census checks and fixed-seed stratified live-page validation.

Issuer and country scope

Include government departments, public authorities, local government, publicly funded research institutes, government-funded research centres and private research units carrying out commissioned national research. A company headquarters or branch location alone is insufficient.

Exclude ordinary business, product and promotional documents from clearly multinational private firms. Preserve them in the source master and retain government-research exceptions with evidence. EU, international and regional organisations are non-national unless a national office, commission, policy jurisdiction or country-specific mandate is demonstrated.

Store published_in_country, issuer_country, country_scope_class, government_research_evidence and country_decision_source separately. `Published in` is provider evidence, not an automatic issuer country.

Known contamination checks

  • Standalone agendas and recruitment notices, with substantive-bundle exceptions.
  • Academic proceedings, theses, patents, media and non-document formats.
  • EU and international organisations misattributed from host location.
  • Multinational private firms misattributed from headquarters or branch location.
  • Kosovo issuer versus Serbia publication-place conflicts.
  • HKG to CHN codebook rule.
  • Substring collisions such as British Columbia and New Zealand Productivity Commission.
  • AI false negatives caused by metadata-only screening.

Full cited examples are in the Policy Commons contamination and country-attribution criteria.
