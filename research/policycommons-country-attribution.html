Policy Commons curation protocol

Measuring country-level attention to AI, beyond formal government reports.

Protocol 3.5 · revised 20 September 2026 · final analytical release v4

Final analytical release v4 is available. Exact RIS–CSV linkage produced 11,306 master records. After applying the revised rules to the full census, 11,093 are eligible documents and 10,725 have supported analysis-country assignments. The country-pair file contains 10,725 unique unit-weight rows across 126 countries. A fixed-seed stratified probability sample of 1,500 across 80 strata passed every Protocol 3.5 conformance check. The conservative unweighted 95% Wilson interval is 99.74–100%. This checks rule application, not original-document semantic accuracy.

Purpose and workflow

Measure AI-related issues, interests and policy activity attributable to countries. The exact organisation within a country is secondary. A producer, publisher, public commissioner or official sponsor can establish a national connection. Different institutional names from the same country do not by themselves invalidate attribution.

Keep AI relevance, AI centrality, document form and country evidence separate. The collection was built from AI-oriented Policy Commons searches, including matches within document text. Therefore, collection provenance is positive inclusion evidence. It does not prove that AI is the main topic. Process local inputs first, then additional iCloud inputs under the same rules. Resolve aliases to avoid counting the same file twice. Preserve original metadata, collection categories and decision histories.

Freeze inputs → parse → deduplicate → screen AI with a high-recall rule → classify whole documents → attribute countries → count → validate.

Paired RIS and CSV source collection

The source corpus is now being enriched with a CSV export for every RIS search page under the same module, year, partition, sort order and page number. CSV contributes provider-supplied publication-country and topic fields, while RIS retains abstracts, identifiers, keywords and richer citation metadata. Collection prioritises complete raw capture and does not spend time evaluating page-level overlap. Final enrichment later joins only records with matching CSV.coi and RIS.ID, checked against the Policy Commons artefact ID; unmatched records are skipped from the enriched output and retained in raw storage.

Read the full code-level reproducibility specification and source snapshot.

1. Freeze inputs

Record original/real paths, SHA-256, format, query/category, retrieval time and cloud hydration status. Preserve the exact search string when it is available. If the RIS export does not contain it, label the provenance as user-reported rather than reconstructing or inventing a query. Parse identical file contents once, preserving all collection memberships. Freeze code, environment, dictionaries, institution/country tables, sampling seed and analytical years (1990–2026). Retain older and undated records in the archive.

2. Parse and check

Export every search page in both RIS and CSV under the identical query, filters, sort order, page size and page number. Preserve both raw formats. Derive observation IDs from file hash and record ordinal. Preserve raw RIS and repeated fields; normalise matching copies only. Extract identifiers, URLs, title, abstract/summary, keywords, dates, publisher, authors, affiliations, language and type. Preserve Policy Commons ‘Matches within the text’ evidence when it was captured. Distinguish provider-generated summaries from original abstracts and label unknown summary provenance explicitly.

Join CSV coi to RIS policy_commons_id after normalising only whitespace, URL prefixes and case. Confirm agreement with the Policy Commons URL where available. Do not join by title, row order or page position. Retain unmatched and conflicting records for review. Store CSV country as published_in_country. Store CSV topics separately as policy_commons_topics, retaining the original semicolon-delimited string and a normalised repeatable list. Do not overwrite RIS KW keywords with topics.

Quarantine shifted fields and unrelated joins. A PDF containing multiple notices is instead a mixed_document: retain qualifying content, record boundaries where possible and do not attribute every component to one agency. Recover missing years from an imprint or publisher record; distinguish publication, revision and retrieval dates. Otherwise retain unresolved status.

3. Deduplicate

Generate candidates from raw-record hashes, all namespaced IDs, DOI, canonical source URLs, exact normalised title, publisher, year and long summary/content hashes. Different Policy Commons IDs can refer to the same original document. Merge automatically only on strong compatible evidence: identical raw records; a shared stable identifier plus matching title; or exact title, publisher, year and substantive summary. Verified common original-source URLs may override different Policy Commons IDs. Title similarity alone triggers review.

Check date type, language, edition and source content before merging. Keep translations, substantive revisions and annual editions separately linked. Select the cleanest compatible record, fill missing fields only, and preserve conflicting values, merge rules and field provenance. Audit both false merges and residual duplicates. Link overlapping gazette extracts so the same AI notice is not counted repeatedly; flag unresolved overlaps.

4. Screen AI broadly

Apply a high-recall rule. A record collected through the documented AI-oriented Policy Commons searches is included by default, even when the exported title, summary and keywords do not show an AI term. Policy Commons may have matched the document body. Collection provenance supports inclusion but does not establish AI centrality. Record terms, fields, full-text-match evidence, query provenance and rule IDs separately.

Policy Commons AI search criteria

  • Explicit AI: artificial intelligence, machine/deep/reinforcement learning, neural networks, natural language processing, computer vision, generative AI, large language models; 인공지능, 기계학습, 머신러닝, 人工智能, 人工智慧, 人工知能.
  • Governance and applications: AI governance, risk, system, policy, ethics, regulation, safety, technology, strategy and standards; substantive applications and societal impacts.
  • Historical or ambiguous: expert systems, knowledge-based systems, machine intelligence, intelligent systems, 지능형, 머신러닝, LLM, GPT and ML require computational or explicit AI context.
  • Alternative meanings: exclude avian influenza, biological neural networks and similar cases only when no independent AI evidence remains.

Use policy_commons_topics as provider-generated topical evidence. It can support AI relevance, domain classification and policy-theme analysis, but does not prove that AI is central to the document and does not replace original-document verification. Preserve RIS keywords, CSV topics and analyst-derived labels in separate fields with separate provenance.

Use separate fields for ai_include, evidence status and scope: central | substantive_application | policy_background | incidental | negative_only | unresolved. Exclude as non_ai only with high-confidence positive evidence of an alternate meaning, mapping/join contamination, or a hit confined to navigation, references or URLs with no AI discussion. Absence of a metadata keyword is not exclusion evidence. If the original is inaccessible, retain it as collection_provenance_only. Log every exclusion with evidence, location, rule version and review date. Rescreen the recoverable population, including exclusions, whenever the dictionary or rule changes.

5. Classify the document as a whole

DocumentTreatment
Standalone thesis, scholarly article, conference paper, patent or logistical event programmeExclude from the analysis view; preserve the decision record.
Policy or commissioned technical report, institutional working paper, substantive statement, recommendation or decisionRetain with supported AI relevance and national connection.
Report with recruitment/event material; gazette containing several noticesRetain as a mixed document. Embedded material alone does not disqualify the whole file.
Standalone meeting, council, committee or board agendaExclude unless the title identifies a substantive bundle such as minutes, supporting documents, a report, policy, strategy, assessment, recommendation, briefing, submission, transcript or official record.
Standalone job profile, vacancy announcement or recruitment-opportunity noticeSeparate auxiliary category, not a policy report. Retain recruitment policy, compliance, evaluation and labour-market analysis.
Standalone audio, video, image or tableExclude from the document view. Scanned reports remain documents.

Keep uncertain genres flagged. National affiliation does not override the standalone academic-material exclusion.

6. Attribute countries inclusively

Accept a supported national connection without requiring perfect within-country issuer identification. Preserve the relationship used for attribution. Policy Commons CSV exports include a country field that is often absent from RIS. Use the CSV value as provider-supplied publication-country evidence, not as an automatic substitute for issuer or analytical country.

Required RIS and CSV linkage
Join CSV.coi to RIS.policy_commons_id. Preserve published_in_country, issuer_country, target_country, policy_jurisdiction and analysis_country as separate fields. Record country_source and the exact evidence used. If CSV and institutional evidence conflict, retain both and require review.
EvidenceDecision
Policy Commons CSV country / detail-page ‘Published in’Store as published_in_country. Treat as strong provider-supplied evidence for ordinary national and subnational public bodies, subject to conflict checks.
Producer, publisher, public commissioner or official sponsor belongs to the countryAccept that country. Within-country role or naming differences are not an exclusion reason.
Mixed gazette from agencies of the same countryRetain the country and mixed-document label, without claiming a single agency issued all content.
International, EU or multinational organisationDo not convert CSV publication location or headquarters location directly into national attribution. Include a country only for a supported national office, commission, policy jurisdiction or substantive country-specific report.
Language, publication place or author affiliationUse actively as supporting/candidate evidence. Shared languages and translations do not uniquely identify a country.
Storage or mirroring onlyDo not automatically assign the host country. Keep unresolved rather than invent attribution.

Explicit jurisdiction in an issuer name overrides a conflicting publication place. For example, Government of Kosovo is assigned to Kosovo rather than Serbia. National, parliamentary and government library bodies may be actual public issuers, not merely repositories. Official national commissions and domestic branches of international organisations may support national attribution, while supranational services remain separate. Regression checks must also prevent substring collisions such as British Columbia being interpreted as the United Kingdom and New Zealand Productivity Commission as Australia.

For KPMG, BCG, Deloitte and similar firms, use a supported national entity/office, commission or country-focused report. A global brand alone is insufficient; exact intra-country office identification is unnecessary once the national relationship is supported.

Preserve published_in_country for the Policy Commons CSV/detail-page value and issuer_country/CTRY for issuer evidence. Store producer, commissioner, sponsor, target-country and policy-jurisdiction links separately; their qualifying union forms analysis_country. Do not silently relabel publication place or every institutional relationship as issuance. Use ISO alpha-3 codes, evidence references and unresolved flags. Apply evidence in this order: matched CSV/detail-page value, explicit institutional jurisdiction, original-document evidence, then controlled inference. Language and generic place evidence remain supporting signals only.

Actual audited examples
  • EEG machine-learning report: Carnegie Mellon producer and US Army sponsor → United States.
  • Truck-trip tracking report: University of Tennessee and Tennessee transport authorities → United States.
  • Peruvian central-bank working paper stored as “Government of Peru” → Peru.
  • French AI Commission report stored as “Government of France” → France.
  • US gazette combining patent-office AI text and Army notices → United States, with mixed-document and overlap flags.
  • crumble CRAN manual stored under CSIRO → Australian attribution unresolved; hosting alone is insufficient.

7. Count document-country pairs

Maintain broad AI candidates, eligible country-interest documents, and auxiliary/unresolved records. Apply AI and genre rules, select supported national relationships, then retain unique (record_id, analysis_country) pairs with weight 1.

A document linked to Korea and the United States contributes one to each, without percentage shares. Two Korean institutions still contribute one to Korea. Report unique documents and document-country rows separately. Multilingual does not automatically mean multinational.

8. Validate and release

Freeze the sampling frame, rules and seed before inspection. Stratify by period, institution type and error-risk mechanism, including agenda/recruitment exclusions, explicit jurisdiction overrides, provider-country use, issuer fallback and unresolved or supranational cases. Give small high-risk strata a minimum allocation, allocate the remainder by population size, and retain N_h, n_h and N_h/n_h. For an AI-oriented Policy Commons collection, do not routinely re-adjudicate AI relevance: retain the collection-stage AI presumption and record only clear non-AI contamination encountered during review.

Required checks for this and future datasets

  1. Confirm exact RIS–CSV linkage from the normalised Policy Commons ID and artefact ID.
  2. Test standalone agenda and recruitment exclusions, including substantive-bundle exceptions.
  3. Test CSV country against issuer jurisdiction, especially Kosovo and other disputed or conflicting cases.
  4. Test public libraries and national commissions as potential issuers, while keeping true repositories and supranational services separate.
  5. Run named collision tests for British Columbia and New Zealand Productivity Commission, plus newly discovered collisions.
  6. Confirm unique (record_id, analysis_country) rows and unit weights.
  7. Record inaccessible evidence as unverifiable; do not count it as semantically correct.

Apply confirmed deterministic corrections to the entire census, not only the sample. Report the resulting census correction rate separately from sampled conformance. A sample that re-executes the same coded rules measures implementation consistency. It does not estimate independent semantic accuracy unless reviewers inspect original documents without relying on the production labels.

Reconcile additional iCloud inputs under the same rules, then create a new versioned release. Preserve manifests, configuration, executable code/environment, dictionaries, canonical records, source/field links, duplicate decisions, country evidence, analysis membership, validation decisions, stage counts and checksums. Physical deletion is separate from analytical exclusion.

Protocol 3.5 corrected 211 of 11,306 v1 records after applying the discovered rules to every record: 192 document-form decisions and 19 country decisions. The resulting 1.8663% is an exact release-to-release census correction rate, not a sampled error estimate. The expanded sample crossed period, institution type and risk mechanism. All 1,500 records passed the implemented checks; the conservative unweighted Wilson 95% interval for protocol conformance is 99.74–100%. Because the checks re-executed the production rules rather than independently reading every original, semantic accuracy remains unestimated.

The principal corrections were 190 standalone meeting agendas, two standalone recruitment materials, six Government of Kosovo records, 11 public or parliamentary library records and two Canadian Commission for Unesco records. Regression tests also retain the earlier fixes for 59 British Columbia and seven New Zealand Productivity Commission substring collisions.
